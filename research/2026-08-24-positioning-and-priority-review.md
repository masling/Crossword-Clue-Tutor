# Positioning and priority review — 2026-08-24

## Decision

Do not pivot away from exact daily clue pages. They are already the acquisition wedge. Reframe the product around one promise:

> Fast enough for today's clue; clear enough to help with the next one.

The durable advantage is not “progressive hints” alone. Several competitors now offer hints or explanations. The advantage must be the combination of timely selected coverage, verified clue-answer relationships, spoiler control, independently reviewed explanations, answer-meaning pages, ambiguity data, and a privacy-conscious no-account workflow.

## Current strengths

1. **Search intent is already proven.** Exact daily clue pages, not the homepage, generate almost all current visibility.
2. **The experience has a coherent learning loop.** Hint → reveal → why it fits → next clue → save or daily practice.
3. **The editorial boundary is credible.** Selected clues, original explanations, no full grid, visible review dates, and feedback.
4. **The data can become a moat.** Official-source local clue history, answer meanings, clue families, and ambiguity candidates can improve ranking and tool quality over time.
5. **There are legitimate linkable assets.** Teacher guide, worksheet, classroom solver, and ambiguity dataset are better outreach material than a generic solver homepage.

## Current weaknesses

1. **The domain is new and has little external authority.** A position-6 result from an unfamiliar brand will lose clicks to established puzzle sites.
2. **The reviewed public corpus is still small.** It cannot yet compete with large clue databases on broad solver recall.
3. **Publication timing is decisive.** Competitors can publish before search demand peaks; a high-quality page arriving late misses most of the daily window.
4. **Feature-level differentiation is narrowing.** Progressive hints and explanations are now common claims. The site must prove review quality and continuity rather than merely state them.
5. **The traffic target is aggressive.** At 0.46% CTR, 500 visitors require roughly 109,000 impressions. At 2.2% CTR, they require about 22,700 impressions. Ranking and coverage growth matter more than cosmetic redesign.

## Revised two-week execution mix

### 1. Acquisition: 60%

- Keep NYT Mini, NYT daily, LA Times, and USA TODAY as the core sources. Do not add another publication until all four are consistently covered on release.
- Publish a small selected set for each newly verified source date as soon as official source data and exact pairs are available.
- Prioritize clues likely to trigger searches: distinctive quoted phrases, question-mark wordplay, cultural entities, unfamiliar foreign words, ambiguous blanks, and clues with multiple plausible fills.
- Keep titles exact and clue-led. The unique clue-led description deployment on 2026-08-24 needs at least 72 hours before another metadata rewrite.
- Use the latest-24-hours GSC query list after publication to find missed variants and undercovered pages, not to gate same-day coverage.

### 2. CTR and ranking: 15%

- Review only landing pages with at least 20 impressions, average position 8 or better, and zero clicks after data finalizes.
- Separate rank problems from snippet problems. Pages at positions 9–11 need stronger ranking/internal authority before title experiments.
- Compare cohorts, not isolated hours: clue-led descriptions deployed after the current export, so the next decision point is no earlier than 2026-08-27.
- Preserve the answer outside search snippets. The click promise is “hint first, answer and explanation inside,” not a zero-click answer in the SERP.

### 3. Retention and product: 15%

- Measure `answer_reveal`, `solver_submit`, `return_path_click`, `save_clue`, and `daily_clinic_complete` on search landing sessions.
- Treat a second page, a solver submission, a saved clue, or a completed clinic as the first useful activation signal.
- Keep the latest clinic, saved clues, recently viewed clues, and installable-app entry visible but secondary to the answer task.
- Improve solver recall from the private source database only through reviewed candidates; never expose the complete stored clue list as a public answer dump.

### 4. Authority and links: 10%

- Send two or three high-fit pitches per week, each mapped to one real asset and audience.
- Favor editorial mentions, classroom-resource lists, constructor resources, and correction/citation opportunities over generic directories.
- Track live links separately from promises and sent messages. Do not count an email or a direct visit as backlink success.

## Operating gates

| Signal | Action |
| --- | --- |
| New source date and verified pairs | Publish selected current clues immediately. |
| ≥20 impressions, position ≤8, zero clicks after finalized data | Review title/snippet and SERP competition once. |
| Position 9–15 with impressions | Improve internal links, source hub, and coverage; do not repeatedly rewrite metadata. |
| Search landing gets clicks but no second-page/tool action | Improve on-page continuation and answer-to-next-clue transition. |
| No impressions | Do not polish the page repeatedly; improve discovery, source timing, or choose a stronger query family. |
| Outreach sent but no reply | Follow up once after 7–10 days only when the asset remains relevant. |

## What to stop or defer

- Stop broad batches of generic answer-meaning pages without search, source, or solver evidence.
- Stop treating “explanation quality” as a reason to select a clue before search demand exists; quality remains a publication requirement.
- Defer another visual redesign. The main constraint is authority, speed, rank, and coverage—not homepage aesthetics.
- Defer adding more publishers until current official adapters operate reliably across the four monitored sources.
- Do not judge the 2026-08-24 metadata fix from a report window that predates deployment.
