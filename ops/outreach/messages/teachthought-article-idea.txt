“How Crossword Clues Teach Evidence-Based Vocabulary Inference”

The article would present a practical 15-minute routine in which students treat a clue as a compact evidence problem instead of recalling an answer from memory. The proposed 700–900 word article would include:

1. How grammar, punctuation, abbreviations, and answer length constrain meaning.
2. A progressive-hint routine that preserves productive struggle.
3. How trusted crossing letters turn guesses into evidence-based revisions.
4. A short student explanation frame: “The answer fits because …”
5. One ready-to-use classroom example plus differentiation for multilingual learners.

The central takeaway is useful without any particular platform: crossword clues can make polysemy, part of speech, connotation, and inference visible in a small amount of class time.
